The Stanford Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, together with the Departments of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, cordially invite you to submit your papers to ECJ, the first-ever EE/CS journal magazine at Stanford, due to launch in late May. ECJ will showcase the top and latest research being done at the union of electrical engineering and computer science disciplines by Stanford graduate and undergraduate students. Similar publications already exist at other universities, such as MIT and our friends across the Bay.

ECJ is a professor-reviewed technical journal magazine available both online and in print, serving to 1) create awareness across disciplines within the campus technical community, 2) prepare current researchers for the publication and review process of larger journals, and 3) inspire future research by Stanford community members. Moreover, students submitting the best papers will receive scholarships worth up to $750.


• Increase the exposure of your groundbreaking research project.
• Sizeable scholarship grants for the top papers.
• ECJ has a rapid review process. Typical journals have long turn around times ranging from months to years.
• Rare opportunity for research paper to be reviewed by top EE and CS professors in the world.
• Make an impact on campus and the global technical community. Work will be displayed for peers and faculty viewing in the Terman Engineering Library.


E-mail submissions in either word or PDF format to ieee@ee.stanford.edu by Sunday, April 18th at midnight. We are looking for two paper formats: short papers and long papers.


Short papers -- such as those for CURIS or REU project, or early stage-research -- should be 2-4 pages and can include broad survey papers as well as more specific technical submissions.


Long papers should be 6-12 pages and can include excerpts from longer papers submitted for other journals. Feel free to modify past presentations, publications, and technical writings you have submitted for classes, other publications, or symposiums for submission.

Long papers will be read by more high-profile reviewers. We expect most graduate student (particularly Ph.D.) submissions to fall in this category.
